Using IIDS, RCDS, or GBRC resources

You submit through your department or college — and the proposal builds on institute computing, genomics, or people. Four steps, in the order you'll hit them.

Plan it

Talk to the core director before you write. Consultation is free: what you need, whether the cores can do it, and roughly what it would cost.

Budget it

Core directors help develop a cost estimate for the proposed work — free. The rates themselves are published by ORED and, for genomics, iLab; university budget rates (F&A, fringe) live with ORED. Linked, never restated.

Describe it

What reviewers read: facilities and resources statements for RCDS, GBRC, the IIDS Administrative Core, and NKN; letters of support from core directors, who can join as key personnel when appropriate; and the data management plan, which RCDS helps draft during proposal development.

Declare it

Required If the work will use IIDS infrastructure, personnel, or resources: check the box when you submit — the walkthrough below shows exactly where it is. It costs your college and department nothing, and it cannot be added later.

What counts — examples, not an exhaustive list: sequencing, sample preparation, and bioinformatics from GBRC; high performance computing, storage, and development from RCDS. Unsure whether your project qualifies? Ask before you submit, not after.

Submitting a proposal through IIDS

IIDS prepares and submits the proposal and acts as your liaison with the Office of Sponsored Programs, then administers the award if funded.

Criteria

The IIDS Director and relevant staff evaluate every request against three things — intellectual alignment with the IIDS mission, reliance on IIDS infrastructure and personnel, and current and future staff capacity. If yes, IIDS submits for you. If no, you submit through your department — and the resources track above still applies.

How IIDS evaluates a submission request →

Request

Email the Program Manager five things: the RFA (PDF or link) · the submission deadline · PIs and Co-PIs including external collaborators · a 2–5 sentence summary · a 2–5 sentence justification of why submission through IIDS is appropriate. The last two are what the request is judged on.

Submitting a proposal through IIDS →

Timeline

A minimum of three weeks before the sponsor's deadline; four if collaborative or with subawards or contracts. Ask early: alignment and reliance do not change between now and your deadline, but capacity does — a proposal that aligns perfectly can still be declined because the team is already committed.

What you get

Submitting through IIDS means the institute's admin core works the proposal with you:

  • RFA and program-guideline review
  • Budgets and budget justifications
  • Supporting documentation prepared, tracked, and uploaded — current and pending support, biosketches, letters of support
  • Subaward documentation
  • Uploads to VERAS, Grants.gov, Research.gov, and FastLane
  • Coordination with the Office of Sponsored Programs and ORFD
  • Proposal pre-review — IIDS pays stipends to qualified external reviewers to read your proposal before it goes out
  • Teaching release funds for faculty working on large programmatic proposals
  • Art and graphic design for grant figures
  • And after the award: full administration — purchasing, travel, hiring, budget changes. The how-tos are collected in the Guide: Managing Your Award with IIDS →

Pre-review and teaching release are budget and calendar decisions, not last-minute ones — ask about both early.
